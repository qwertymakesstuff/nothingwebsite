# FAKEOS — Game Design

## Pitch
You switch on a computer in October 2013. It comes with an internet: search engines, video sites, forums and social networks that have their own companies, celebrities, feuds, memes and buried secrets, built up over the years before you arrived. You explore it like you explored the real thing at that age: following links, falling down rabbit holes, finding the odd corner nobody talks about.

## Pillars
1. **Nostalgia** — 2009–2015 desktop and web design: glossy chrome, sidebars, view counts, "submitted 3 years ago".
2. **Comedy** — toaster reviewers, microwave lasagna, a man and his gravy boat, a cat who does not care.
3. **Authenticity** — people write differently, argue, are boring sometimes, and remember each other.
4. **Weirdness** — rarely, things don't add up: a channel whose video titles are future dates, a door that shouldn't open, the same minute (4:17) turning up where it shouldn't. Rarely explained.

## Player experience (current build)
- Boot, set a user name, land on a desktop. A Welcome Center explains the computer in-world.
- Browse Findr, Vidder, Threadit and Blink in the Skylark browser: search, watch, read, vote, subscribe, follow, post status updates.
- Notice people across sites (Danny cooks on Vidder, argues about carbonara on Threadit, posts potluck photos on Blink).
- Piece together storylines from scattered sources: the Volt Rush sponsorship scandal, the Fawn Field palette feud, the Port Wexley power cut, the Lantern Tape.

## Rules for writing (summary — see CONTENT_BIBLE.md)
- Never call the internet fictional, simulated or fake in-world.
- Profanity allowed, used naturally. Not every comment is a joke.
- Fourth-wall anomalies: extraordinarily rare, never confirmed, never explained.
